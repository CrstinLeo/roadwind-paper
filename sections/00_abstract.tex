\begin{abstract}
Wind changes the air drag of heavy trucks, but the roadside wind is rarely known: weather stations measure at \SI{10}{m} in open terrain, whereas roads run near the ground through forest and valleys. We correct station wind to roadside weather stations with open data and ask what this changes in estimates of the air-drag energy of a tractor--semitrailer. In central Norway (\noRef{} reference and \noTrv{} roadside stations, 23 months), the nearest station overestimates the mean roadside wind by \noSpdBiasAbsRaw\,\%. Height and roughness conversion, a site factor per station, an exposure model dominated by the forest fraction within \SI{1}{km}, and the combination of all stations reduce the median hourly speed error from \noCvRaw{} to \noCvMed~m\,s$^{-1}$ out of sample (\noHoldMed~m\,s$^{-1}$ in a 2025 hold-out). This beats rescaled nearest-station and neighbouring-road-station wind and matches the MET Nordic analysis; MET Nordic rescaled by one constant is as good, and adding the exposure model to it gives the lowest errors. At \SI{80}{km.h^{-1}} the observed roadside wind raises the air-drag energy at the station sites by \enAddNo\,\%, mostly through crosswind; plausible height conversions give \enBandNoLo--\enBandNoHi\,\%. The nearest station roughly doubles this penalty and predicts hourly energy worse than ignoring the wind; the corrected wind has a pooled skill of \enSsNoCor{} but beats ignoring the wind at only \enStGainNoCor{} of \enNoStations{} sites. At vehicle height, in eight hours, only MET Nordic predicted the energy better than ignoring the wind, mainly through its wind direction.
\end{abstract}
