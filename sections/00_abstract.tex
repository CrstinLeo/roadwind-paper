\begin{abstract}
Wind changes the air drag of heavy trucks, but the wind on the road is rarely known: weather stations measure at \SI{10}{m} in open terrain, whereas roads run near the ground through forest and valleys. Using only open data, we correct station wind to the roadside and quantify what this means for the air-drag energy of a tractor--semitrailer. In central Norway, \noRef{} reference stations and \noTrv{} roadside stations over two years (with a check in western Sweden) show that stations overestimate the mean roadside wind by \noLadRawBiasAbs\,\%. A correction ladder---height and roughness conversion, a site factor per station, a parsimonious roadside exposure model with trees within \SI{100}{m} and the forest fraction within \SI{1}{km}, and the combination of all stations---reduces the median hourly wind-speed error from \noCvRaw{} to \noCvMed~m\,s$^{-1}$ out of time and out of station. At \SI{80}{km.h^{-1}} the roadside wind raises the air-drag energy on average by \enAddNo\,\%, almost entirely through crosswind, and by up to \enRoadMaxNo\,\% at exposed roads. The nearest station doubles this penalty, ranks roads poorly and predicts hourly energy worse than ignoring the wind (skill \enSsNoRaw). The corrected wind removes the bias, reproduces the road penalties (correlation \enRoadCorrNoCor) and reduces the energy error in strong-wind hours by a third. At vehicle height its drag error is below that of the nearest stations and close to that of the MET Nordic analysis; the remaining gap lies in the wind direction.
\end{abstract}
