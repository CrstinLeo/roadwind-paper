\subsection{Correction ladder}
\label{sec:res-ladder}
Figure~\ref{fig:ladder} follows the hourly wind speed of all pairs of a roadside and a reference station less than \SI{80}{km} apart (\noPairsEighty{} pairs in Norway, \sePairsEighty{} in Sweden) through the steps of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ladder}, with the exposure model evaluated leave-one-station-out. Without correction the reference stations overestimate the roadside wind by about a factor of two in both countries (median pair bias \noLadRawBias\,\% in Norway and \seLadRawBias\,\% in Sweden). The physical steps behave differently in the two landscapes. In Sweden, height and roughness conversion reduce the median pair error from \seLadRaw{} to \seLadRough~m\,s$^{-1}$, because the synoptic stations stand in open terrain and the roadside stations in forest. In Norway, where both networks share the same sensor height and much of the difference is caused by terrain, the roughness conversion changes the error only from \noLadRaw{} to \noLadRough~m\,s$^{-1}$. The site factors slightly increase the median pair error in both countries; they are designed for the combined estimate, where they make the reference stations comparable (Section~\ref{sec:res-sens}). The roadside exposure model gives the largest reduction in both countries, to \noLadExpo~m\,s$^{-1}$ in Norway and \seLadExpo~m\,s$^{-1}$ in Sweden.

\subsection{Skill out of time and out of station}
\label{sec:res-skill}
Table~\ref{tab:skill} summarises the cross-validated skill of the final model, in which every estimate is made for a time block and a roadside station that were not used for fitting. In Norway the median station error of the hourly wind speed falls from \noCvRaw~m\,s$^{-1}$ (nearest reference station) to \noCvMed~m\,s$^{-1}$, a reduction of \noCvRed\,\%, and \noCvBetter{} of \noTrv{} stations improve. The reduction is similar in the leaf-on (\noOnRed\,\%) and the leaf-off season (\noOffRed\,\%); absolute errors are larger in winter, when the wind is stronger. In Sweden the median error falls from \seCvRaw{} to \seCvMed~m\,s$^{-1}$ (\seCvRed\,\%), with \seCvBetter{} of \seTrv{} stations improved. Wind direction improves less: from \noCvDirRaw{} to \noCvDir$^\circ$ in Norway, where valleys and fjords turn the wind strongly, and from \seCvDirRaw{} to \seCvDir$^\circ$ in Sweden. Compared with the reference configuration that was the starting point of the analysis (a single tree scale of \SI{200}{m}, a speed--shelter interaction, direction-dependent site factors and a \SI{60}{km} combination), the final model reduces the error by \noRefSixDiff~$\pm$~\noRefSixSE~m\,s$^{-1}$ in Norway and \seRefSixDiff~$\pm$~\seRefSixSE~m\,s$^{-1}$ in Sweden. Out of time the model underestimates the roadside wind by about \noCvBias\,\% (Norway) and \seCvBias\,\% (Sweden), whereas the bias is close to zero when it is fitted and evaluated on the same hours; this is a regional error that varies with the weather and that a static model cannot remove (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).

\subsection{Roadside exposure model}
\label{sec:res-model}
Table~\ref{tab:coefs} gives the coefficients in the two countries. Within each country they are stable: all terms keep their sign and size when one of the time blocks is left out. Between the countries, three terms agree in sign---the distance to the forest edge, the regional wind speed and the landscape forest fraction---and the direction model turns the roadside wind by about \ang{14} on average and towards the road axis by up to \ang{25}--\ang{34}. The landscape forest fraction is by far the strongest predictor in Norway, where it varies between open mountain plateaus and forested valleys. The near-tree term has opposite signs: in Sweden trees within \SI{100}{m} reduce the roadside wind, whereas in Norway, with the forest fraction of the landscape already in the model, stations with trees close to the road have slightly higher wind than expected. The two tree terms are correlated, and in Norway near trees often mark road cuttings and settlements in otherwise open terrain; the near-tree coefficient should therefore not be interpreted in isolation.

\subsection{Sensitivity to length scales and model choices}
\label{sec:res-sens}
Figure~\ref{fig:sens} shows the change in cross-validated error in Norway when one choice is varied around the final model. The combination of reference stations matters most: using only stations within \SI{20}{km} or \SI{40}{km} increases the error by \noKmTwenty{} and \noKmForty~m\,s$^{-1}$; even \SI{80}{km} is worse than using all stations (\noKmEighty~$\pm$~\noKmEightySE). Weights $d^{-1}$ to $d^{-1.5}$ are best. The landscape forest fraction is essential (\noFarNone~$\pm$~\noFarNoneSE~m\,s$^{-1}$ without it), and taking it in all directions is better than in the upwind sector (\noFarUp~$\pm$~\noFarUpSE). The near-tree radius between \SIrange{50}{500}{m} hardly matters in Norway. One site factor per reference station is better than direction-dependent factors (\noSiteSector~$\pm$~\noSiteSectorSE), while omitting the site factors gives an uncertain result (\noSiteNone~$\pm$~\noSiteNoneSE). The roughness conversion, a speed--shelter interaction and the cross-road wind component change the error by less than \SI{0.01}{m.s^{-1}}. Terrain indices (upwind shelter index, topographic position, relief), tested with a preliminary version of the final model, did not improve the cross-validated error in any combination (changes between $+0.001$ and $+0.008$~m\,s$^{-1}$). In Sweden, omitting the site factors increased the error of the reference configuration by about \SI{0.05}{m.s^{-1}}. In Sweden the parameters of the roughness conversion (footprint length \SIrange{25}{2000}{m}, blending height \SIrange{30}{150}{m}, $z_0/h$ between 0.05 and 0.15) changed the error by at most \SI{0.007}{m.s^{-1}}, and the two shelter scales were confirmed independently (Fig.~\ref{fig:twoscale}): near trees within \SIrange{50}{100}{m} and the forest fraction within about \SI{1}{km}, whereas intermediate radii of \SIrange{300}{500}{m} add nothing.

\subsection{Along-road correlation of exposure}
\label{sec:res-along}
The exposure features change quickly along the road, and almost identically in the two countries (Fig.~\ref{fig:along}a). Their correlation with the value at the station is about 0.84 at \SI{100}{m}, about 0.6--0.7 at \SI{250}{m} and 0.3--0.45 at \SI{1}{km}. Replacing the point features of the Swedish stations by averages over a road segment does not degrade the estimate for segments up to \SI{200}{m} long, whereas longer segments tend to increase the error (Fig.~\ref{fig:along}b).

\subsection{Transfer between the countries}
\label{sec:res-transfer}
With the coefficients fitted in the other country, the median station error is \noWithSe~m\,s$^{-1}$ in Norway (own coefficients \noCvMed, raw \noCvRaw) and \seWithNo~m\,s$^{-1}$ in Sweden (own \seCvMed, raw \seCvRaw). The transferred model keeps a clear advantage over the nearest station in Sweden but only a small one in Norway, mainly because the Norwegian forest-fraction coefficient is much larger than the Swedish one.

\subsection{Vehicle height}
\label{sec:res-vehicle}
Against the van-mounted anemometer (Table~\ref{tab:vehicle}, Fig.~\ref{fig:vehicle}), the station-based estimate has a mean absolute error of \vehModel~m\,s$^{-1}$ over \vehN{} segment passages, smaller than that of the nearest reference station (\vehRefraw) and the nearest road weather station (\vehRoadraw), but larger than that of the MET Nordic analysis (\vehMetnordic). On the open coast of Frøya the nearest road weather station is strongly sheltered and the nearest reference station strongly exposed, and the model corrects most of both errors. In the Dovre valley the model overestimates the wind. The direction error of the station-based estimate is large in Dovre, which also limits the accuracy of the drag estimate: its median relative drag error (\vehDragModel\,\%) is similar to that of the nearest stations, whereas MET Nordic reaches \vehDragMetnordic\,\%.

\subsection{Air drag at the roadside stations}
\label{sec:res-drag}
For a tractor--semitrailer passing the roadside stations in both directions at \SI{80}{km.h^{-1}}, the median station error of the air drag falls from \noDragRaw{} to \noDragModel\,\% in Norway and from \seDragRaw{} to \seDragModel\,\% in Sweden when the corrected estimate replaces the nearest reference station (Table~\ref{tab:skill}).
