\begin{abstract}
Wind is a first-order term in the air drag of heavy vehicles, but the wind on the road is rarely measured. Synoptic stations measure at \SI{10}{m} in open terrain; roads run near the ground through forest and valleys. Using only open observations and geodata, we explain and correct the change of near-surface wind from synoptic to roadside weather stations in central Norway (\noTrv{} roadside and \noRef{} reference stations, 2024--2025) and western Sweden (\seTrv{} and \seRef{} stations, \seHours{} hours). The correction ladder comprises height conversion, roughness conversion with roughness lengths from laser-scanned object heights, a site factor per reference station, and a parsimonious roadside exposure model with trees within \SI{100}{m}, the distance to the forest edge, the regional wind speed and the forest fraction within \SI{1}{km}; the estimates of all reference stations are then combined. Out of sample, the median station error of the hourly wind speed falls from \noCvRaw{} to \noCvMed~m\,s$^{-1}$ in Norway and from \seCvRaw{} to \seCvMed~m\,s$^{-1}$ in Sweden, and the median air-drag error of a tractor--semitrailer at \SI{80}{km.h^{-1}} from \noDragRaw{} to \noDragModel\,\% and from \seDragRaw{} to \seDragModel\,\%. Forest shelters roads at two distinct scales; combining stations matters most. The model form transfers between the countries; part of the coefficients is regional. At vehicle height, the estimate beats the nearest stations overall but not the MET Nordic analysis, because of direction errors in mountain terrain. Exposure decorrelates along roads over about \SI{250}{m}, which bounds useful segment lengths.
\end{abstract}
