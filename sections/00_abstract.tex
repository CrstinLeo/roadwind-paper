\begin{abstract}
Wind is a first-order term in the air drag of heavy vehicles, yet the wind a vehicle meets on the road is rarely measured. Synoptic stations report wind at \SI{10}{m} over open terrain, whereas roads run at a few metres above ground through forest, cuttings and settlements. We quantify how near-surface wind changes from synoptic stations of the Swedish Meteorological and Hydrological Institute (SMHI) to \prelim{133} roadside stations of the Swedish Transport Administration (Trafikverket, \SI{6}{m}) in western Sweden, and explain the change with open geodata. The transfer is decomposed into a correction ladder: (i) logarithmic height conversion, (ii) two-step roughness conversion through a blending height with roughness lengths derived from laser-scanned object heights, (iii) removal of persistent, direction-dependent site factors of each synoptic station estimated from multi-year records, and (iv) a parsimonious roadside exposure model with upwind tree cover at two spatial scales, distance to the forest edge, wind speed and a stability proxy. Wind direction is corrected for channelling along the road axis. Evaluated out of time and out of station, the median absolute error of the hourly wind speed falls from \prelim{1.75} to \prelim{0.7}~m\,s$^{-1}$ and the bias from \prelim{$-57\%$} to \prelim{$\pm 10\%$}. Near-road trees within \prelim{about 100~m} and the forest fraction within \prelim{about 1~km} act as two distinct shelter scales; the local part of the exposure decorrelates along the road over \prelim{about 250~m}, which sets an upper bound for the length of road segments in vehicle-based studies. \todo{one sentence on the implication for crosswind air-drag error once computed.}
\end{abstract}
